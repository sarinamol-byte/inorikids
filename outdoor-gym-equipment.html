FAQ

Outdoor gym questions

What is an open gym or outdoor gym?

An outdoor gym, also called an open gym, is a set of fixed, weather-proof exercise stations installed in a park, society or campus. Most stations use body weight as resistance, so they need no power, weights or supervision and are free for residents to use.

What outdoor gym equipment should a housing society install?

A good society open gym combines a few strength stations (press and pull movements), one or two cardio stations such as a cross-trainer, a calisthenics bar frame and at least one senior-friendly balance or mobility station, placed along a walking track on rubber or turf surfacing.

How much space does an outdoor gym need?

Each station needs its own footprint plus clear space for movement around it. A compact society open gym with six to eight stations typically fits in 100 to 200 square metres; a park fitness zone can be much larger. We plan layouts using the safety-zone principles of EN 16630, the European standard for outdoor fitness equipment.

Is outdoor gym equipment suitable for senior citizens?

Yes, if you choose the right stations. Senior ranges focus on balance, flexibility and gentle strength with low step-on heights and handholds.

How do outdoor gym machines hold up to sun and rain?

The stations are built for permanent outdoor installation, with powder-coated frames designed for long outdoor life. We recommend shade where possible and include care guidance at handover.
