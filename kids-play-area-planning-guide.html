How to plan a kids play area for a society or school

A practical checklist for developers, RWAs, school managements and architects in India, from the first site measurement to handover.

Quick answer

To plan a kids play area: define the age groups and expected users, measure the site and allow safety zones of about 1.5–2 m around each piece, decide on the design and whether it needs a landmark piece, lay impact-absorbing surfacing to the right depth, add shade and parent seating, and confirm warranty and after-sales support before you order.

1. Know who will play

Count the flats or students the area will serve and the age mix. Most Indian societies need a toddler zone (ages 1–5) and a junior zone (ages 5–12). Schools plan by class group. If teenagers will use the space, add climbing, a ninja-style trail or an open gym nearby.

2. Measure the site and its safety zones

Every piece of equipment needs its own footprint plus a clear safety zone, typically at least 1.5–2 m on each side and more in front of slides and swings. Note slopes, drainage, trees, underground services and whether the site is on a podium slab, which affects anchoring and load.

3. Decide what the play area should do for the project

An everyday amenity for residents needs good play value, age zones and shade. A play area that also sells the project, in brochures and on site visits, benefits from a landmark centrepiece designed around the project's theme. Many projects combine both: one sculptural piece surrounded by a complete play area.

4. Give your designer the right brief

Share the site plan, the area available, levels and existing trees, the expected users, and any theme or product you have in mind. A good play area designer will turn this into a layout with safety zones, equipment, surfacing and shade before anything is ordered.

5. Get the surfacing right

Surfacing is a safety system, not a finish. Its depth must match the critical fall height of the equipment above it. Poured EPDM rubber and rubber tiles are the usual choices; artificial grass needs a shock pad underneath. Never install play equipment over concrete, paving or tiles.

6. Plan for heat, rain and shade

Indian summers make shade essential. Use trees, pergolas or shade structures over the busiest equipment and seating, choose lighter surfacing colours, and plan drainage so the area dries fast after monsoon rain.

7. Think about the adults

Parents and caregivers stay longer when there is shaded seating with a clear view of the play area. Benches, a pavilion or an open gym alongside turn a play area into a community space.

8. Confirm certification, installation and warranty

Ask for the equipment's safety certification (for example EN 1176 or ASTM), who installs it, what the warranty covers and how spare parts are supplied. Our play equipment carries EN 1176 and ASTM international safety certifications and Korea's KC certification for children's products. Every installation is planned with safety zones and impact-absorbing surfacing matched to the critical fall height of each piece.

9. Plan maintenance from day one

Agree a simple inspection routine: a quick visual check every week, a fuller check of fixings, moving parts and surfacing every few months, and an annual inspection.

FAQ

Play area planning questions

How do you plan a kids play area for a housing society?

Start with who will use it (ages and numbers), then measure the available site, allow for safety zones around each piece, decide whether you want a landmark centrepiece, specify safety surfacing to the right depth, add shade and seating for parents, and agree warranty and after-sales terms before ordering.

What age zones should a society play area have?

At least two: a toddler zone for ages 1–5 with low platforms and small slides, and a junior zone for ages 5–12 with taller towers, climbing and faster slides. Keeping them apart reduces collisions and lets each zone be designed to the right fall height.

What is the safest surface for a kids play area?

Impact-absorbing surfacing laid to the depth required for the critical fall height of the equipment above it. Poured EPDM rubber and rubber tiles are the most common in India because they are durable, drain well and need little upkeep. Hard surfaces such as concrete or tiles should never be used under play equipment.

How much shade does a play area need in India?

As much as you can give it. Plan trees, pergolas or shade sails over the most-used equipment and seating, choose lighter surfacing colours, and orient slides away from direct afternoon sun, since metal and plastic surfaces can get very hot.
